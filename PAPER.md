## Introduction

The problem. Almost everybody speaks worse in front of a class than in a normal conversation. We rush, we say "um" between thoughts, and we look at the screen or at the floor instead of the people. Nobody notices this while speaking, and the feedback comes after the talk, from a teacher, when it is too late to change anything.

Why it is hard. A mirror shows your face but not how fast you speak. A friend can listen, but a friend cannot count words per minute, "umm" or the seconds you spent looking away, and classmates are busy with their own talks. Phone recorders give a video that nobody wants to watch and count by hand afterwards.

What we did. We built Presentation Coach, a web page that runs at the webcam and measures a rehearsal while it happens: how many words you say per minute, how many filler words ("umm", "uh", "ээ", "ну") you use, and a 1–10 score for eye contact from face tracking. The result line at the end says what to change before the next run: slow down, pause instead of "umm", or look at the camera more.
